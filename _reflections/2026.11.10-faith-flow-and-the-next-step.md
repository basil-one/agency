There are times in life when things seem to flow.

An idea takes shape. A conversation opens a possibility. Something that once seemed impossible becomes imaginable. We find ourselves moving with purpose, perhaps even helping shape a different future.

And then there are times when life doesn't cooperate.

An opportunity disappears. A relationship takes a different course. Our bodies remind us of their limitations.

Life is like a box of chocolates, Forrest Gump famously observed. You never know what you're going to get.

Except sometimes you bite into what looks like chocolate and discover a chocolate-covered rock.

What then?

## When Flow Disappears
Much of my life has involved exploring how change happens.

Through systems thinking, Aikido, philosophy, and organizational change, I've learned to examine constraints, question assumptions, and work with forces rather than simply resist them.

Across these experiences, I keep returning to a question:

**How do we participate meaningfully in life when we cannot control what happens next?**

It's relatively easy to appreciate a philosophy when life is going well.

The harder test comes when we're exhausted, disappointed, physically uncomfortable, or alone.

I've wondered whether I simply needed a better mindset, a stronger spiritual practice, or a clearer vision of the future.

But hardship isn't always a problem of perspective.

Sometimes the constraints are real.

A body needs medical care. Another person makes choices we cannot control. An organization resists change.

No amount of positive thinking makes those realities disappear. And not every difficult experience contains a hidden opportunity.

Sometimes life is simply hard.

I don't want a philosophy that only works when things are going well.

I want one that can accompany me through the difficult stretches, too.

## Faith Without Certainty
I've often noticed the comfort some people find in believing God is looking out for them. That even when circumstances seem overwhelming, they aren't walking alone.

I appreciate the power of that faith, though I remain something of an agnostic.

My own relationship with spirituality is less easily categorized.

I often read a passage from the *Tao Te Ching*, or open *The Art of Peace*. These writings don't necessarily tell me what to do. They help me recognize where I am.

Some days I can relax into their wisdom. Other days I notice how difficult that is.

Perhaps faith doesn't have to begin with certainty about how the universe works.

Perhaps it can begin with a willingness to remain in relationship with life, even when we don't understand what's happening.

Not faith that everything will turn out as we wish.

But faith that another step may become possible, even when we cannot yet see it.

## Footprints in the Sand
My stepfather introduced me to the Christian poem *Footprints in the Sand* during a difficult period in my life.

In the poem, someone looks back over their life and wonders why, during the hardest times, there was only one set of footprints.

The response is that those were the times they were being carried.

My stepfather found comfort in Christ, and the poem meant something to him.

He read it at his father's eulogy.

Years later, after he passed, I read it at his casting ceremony.

The same words, carried across generations, through different experiences of loss.

I've been sitting with that image again.

My own father died when I was young. I remember moments when he offered guidance, when he understood where I was and helped me find my way.

After he died, there were people around me. People who cared.

But I also remember having to send up a signal when I needed help, hoping someone would notice, understand, and respond.

Sometimes they did.

Perhaps that's why the footprints image moves me.

The possibility of being accompanied without having to explain why accompaniment is needed. Of being understood without first having to make the case.

I don't know whether there's a divine presence carrying us through life's difficult passages.

But I know the experience of looking back and wondering how I managed to take the next step.

Was it perseverance? People who loved me? Experience? Chance? Grace?

Perhaps some combination.

I don't know.

And maybe I don't need to know.

## The Footprints We Leave
Lately I've been thinking not only about the footprints that carried me, but those I'm leaving behind.

My children's mother once told me she wished she could have known my father.

I understand that wish.

I knew him as my father, through memories and moments we shared. But I have little access to his inner world.

What did he wonder about? What frightened him? What sustained him through hardship?

I can only imagine.

When I listen to Alan Watts or read someone's deeply introspective writing, I sometimes feel I can encounter something of that person's inner life beyond what a biography might reveal.

I've come to recognize that my own writing may offer something similar to my children.

Not because I have life figured out.

Quite the opposite.

These reflections are records of my inquiries, of how I've tried to make sense of life while living it.

My mother has left something of herself in her writing, too. In [Nest](https://garden.basil.one/nest), her reflections on nature, the changing seasons, and her relationship with God offer a glimpse into her inner world.

Perhaps someday, at my own casting ceremony, someone will read something I've written.

Not because the words provide answers, but because they offer a glimpse into the person who lived them.

I don't need anyone to teach my children what these things mean.

I'd simply like them to know they exist, why they mattered to me, and that they're free to explore them in their own way.

Perhaps someday they'll find something that resonates.

Or perhaps they won't.

Either is okay.

**These are my footprints, not a path they are obligated to follow.**

## The Next Step
I began this inquiry wondering how to reliably experience flow.

But perhaps flow cannot be made permanent.

In Aikido, we learn to meet changing forces with appropriate movement. Sometimes we enter. Sometimes we turn. Sometimes we create distance.

Life seems much the same.

Sometimes we walk. Sometimes we rest. Sometimes we need help getting back up.

Flow may be those periods when our intentions, capabilities, and circumstances come together.

Faith may be what sustains us when that coherence disappears.

And integration may be how we make sense of what we've experienced, discovering what we can learn, what we must accept, and what possibilities remain.

Writing is one way I practice that. It doesn't necessarily change my circumstances, but it can change my relationship with them.

Sometimes that opens a move I couldn't see before.

Maybe that's where faith, flow, and integration meet.

Not in a promise that life will be easy.

Not in a guarantee that everything happens for a reason.

But in a willingness to remain engaged with life as it unfolds.

I think about those footprints in the sand.

I don't know whether I was walking, being carried, or somehow both.

I only know that I am here.

And there is another step to take.
