Sometimes I catch myself taking inventory of my life.

The relationships that didn't endure. The friendships that faded. The opportunities that passed. The things I thought I would have accomplished by now.

And then come the *shoulds*.

I should be further along. Things should have worked out differently. I should have more to show for all these years.

It's easy to compare our lives with what we imagined they would become, or with the lives other people appear to be living.

Sometimes these comparisons arise from real losses. Other times, we find ourselves crossing a threshold, discovering that an old understanding of our lives no longer quite fits.

Some losses are clear. Others are ambiguous. Something has changed, but we haven't yet made sense of what it means.

I've noticed that when I begin measuring my life this way, I lose my center.

## Returning to Center

In Aikido, we practice meeting conflict without becoming consumed by it.

One of the principles we return to is *relax completely*.

Not collapse. Not surrender. Not disengage.

Release unnecessary tension while remaining present and responsive.

I'm learning to recognize something similar in everyday life.

When I become caught in comparison, expectation, or struggling against what has happened, my attention narrows. I become less available to what's actually happening.

When I'm [off-center](https://circle.basil.one/lenses/paradigm/), I can become caught in defending what I believe should be, resisting what is, or trying to control what happens next.

Sometimes noticing that is enough to begin returning to center.

## Life as Inquiry

**Life is an inquiry, not an accumulation.**

For me, this isn't a conclusion about how life should be lived. It's a stance I can return to.

It doesn't mean relationships, accomplishments, commitments, or possessions don't matter. Nor does it mean denying loss, abandoning aspirations, or pretending difficult circumstances are somehow good.

It means remembering that the measure of my life isn't simply what I've accumulated, or what remains.

Instead of asking where I should be, I can become curious about where I am.

What's happening?

What am I learning?

What is changing?

What remains within my ability to influence?

What might be possible from here?

These questions don't necessarily resolve anything. But they can change how I participate.

Sometimes they help me recognize something I need to accept. Sometimes they reveal a choice I hadn't considered. Sometimes they simply help me make room for what I'm experiencing.

## The Practice of Returning

I don't expect to remain centered.

There will be more comparisons, disappointments, unfinished relationships, and moments when life doesn't resemble what I imagined.

There will also be discoveries, meaningful encounters, and opportunities I couldn't have anticipated.

I can continue making commitments, building relationships, and working toward things that matter without making their accumulation or permanence the measure of my life.

And when I find myself taking inventory again, I can notice.

Perhaps relax a little.

Return to inquiry.

I don't need to have life figured out to participate in it.

**Life is an inquiry, not an accumulation.**
