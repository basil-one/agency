How much of our lives is shaped by our relationship with conflict?

The things we leave unsaid. The ideas we don't pursue. The relationships we hesitate to deepen. The opportunities we walk away from.

Sometimes that's good discernment. Other times, I wonder whether we're simply unfamiliar with how to navigate what might happen.

## Learning to Work With Tension

One of the things I enjoy about practicing Aikido is developing a different relationship with incoming force.

There is a time to ground. A time to move. A time to enter. A time to create distance.

As our sensitivity develops, we become more capable of working with what's happening, increasingly without having to think about it.

I notice this translating into my everyday experience of conflict, negotiation, and everything in between.

When something is on the line, I notice a shift within myself. My energy changes. My experience of the other person changes. What I perceive to be possible changes.

Can I recognize that shift? Can I recover a wider awareness while the tension is still present?

Can that, too, become something I develop through practice?

## Learning to Swim

Imagine experiencing the world without knowing how to swim.

An entire dimension of life might remain largely inaccessible to us. Not because it has nothing to offer, but because we haven't developed the capacity to participate in it.

Does learning to swim eliminate the dangers of water?

Of course not.

And yet, something that once evoked fear can become a source of enjoyment, exploration, and even adventure.

In my experience, developing conflict intelligence opens a similar world. I've noticed it in others as well.

We become more willing to enter difficult conversations, explore our differences, challenge one another, and deepen our relationships.

We may even discover that some tension is worth cultivating.

## What Becomes Possible Together

Think about how it feels to come through a storm with another human being and find ourselves closer than we were before.

Something has changed between us. We've experienced difficulty together, discovered things about ourselves and one another, and developed a greater confidence in what our relationship can hold.

There is a sweetness to that experience that seems inseparable from the bitterness we've moved through together.

It brings a certain aliveness to a relationship.

Imagine cultivating that capacity within a family, a community of practice, or an organization.

Where disagreement doesn't immediately threaten belonging. Where challenging an idea is part of developing it. Where tension can become a source of understanding, creativity, and connection.

Not every conflict will be transformative. Not every relationship should endure.

Part of developing intelligence around conflict is learning when to lean in, when to hold our ground, and when to let go.

**How does our world open up as we become more skillful at working with tension?**
