When we think of leadership, we often imagine someone at the center. Someone who provides direction, makes decisions, and brings others into alignment.

But some of the most meaningful leadership I've experienced hasn't looked like that.

Consider a group of musicians playing together. Someone might conduct, setting the tempo and guiding the performance. But musicians can also improvise, listening and responding to one another, finding their way through the music without anyone directing every note.

Both can produce something remarkable.

What changes is how they organize themselves.

## Three Ways Order Emerges

I've been reflecting on three ways people establish order together.

**Hierarchy** organizes through authority and structure. Like a conductor leading an orchestra, someone establishes direction and coordinates the parts. This can be especially useful when clarity, consistency, or decisive action matters.

**Anarchy** allows order to emerge through autonomy and voluntary association. Think of musicians gathering for an informal jam session. Nobody needs to be in charge. People contribute freely, finding their own ways to participate.

**Heterarchy** organizes through relationships and mutual influence. Think of a jazz ensemble, where leadership shifts as the music unfolds. One musician introduces an idea, another responds, and the others adjust. Each retains their own voice while contributing to something larger.

These aren't mutually exclusive. An orchestra still requires musicians who listen and adapt, and a jazz ensemble still needs enough [structure to hold the music together](/reflections/culture-is-infrastructure/).

The distinction is where order comes from and how people participate in creating it.

## The Energy We Bring

There's another dimension to leadership: the energy we bring to a situation.

I've been exploring this through an [Energy Awareness Reflection](https://circle.basil.one/lenses/energy/), one of the Leadership Lenses developed for [Circle3: Three Guiding Moves for Leading Difficult Change](https://circle.basil.one/paper).

Circle3 is a lightweight approach to helping people develop shared understanding and move forward when change becomes stuck. Its Leadership Lenses offer ways to notice what's happening beneath the surface of our interactions.

The Energy Awareness Reflection invites us to recognize different expressions of energy in ourselves and others.

Sometimes we need **Driver energy** to establish direction and bring something to completion. Sometimes **Organizer energy** helps us create structure and a reliable rhythm. **Collaborator energy** connects people and perspectives, while **Visionary energy** opens possibilities we haven't yet considered.

These aren't fixed identities. We can access and cultivate different energies depending on the circumstances, and each can become counterproductive when overused.

I've noticed this in myself. I enjoy exploring possibilities and bringing different perspectives together. But when ambiguity lingers without producing insight, or important loose ends remain unresolved, I feel a strong impulse to establish direction and bring things to closure.

Neither tendency is inherently better. The question is whether the energy fits what the situation needs.

Like musicians, we need to recognize when to explore, when to establish a rhythm, when to build tension, and when to bring a phrase to resolution.

## Coherence Without Control

I've come to appreciate leadership less as a particular style and more as an [adaptive practice](/reflections/we-walk-together/).

It's not simply about recognizing the energy we bring, but noticing how that energy interacts with the people and circumstances around us.

Sometimes someone needs to decide. Sometimes people need room to explore independently. Sometimes the most useful thing we can do is help people connect and discover their own way forward.

This has also led me to distinguish between alignment and coherence.

Alignment can suggest everyone pointing in the same direction. Coherence allows for something more dynamic.

Think again of musicians improvising together. They aren't playing the same notes or necessarily following a predetermined arrangement. They're listening, responding, and adjusting while retaining their individual voices.

Their differences aren't obstacles to the music. They're part of what makes it possible.

Perhaps leadership is something similar.

**Leadership cultivates coherence among [autonomous, interconnected agents](/reflections/what-i-mean-by-human-layer-engineering/).**

Not by requiring everyone to agree, surrender their agency, or depend on a central authority, but by helping people find ways to move meaningfully together.

The aim isn't [control or consensus](/reflections/principle-survives-disagreement/). It's coherent action that preserves agency.

And perhaps one of the most important leadership moves is recognizing when to step forward, when to step aside, and when to let something emerge between us.
