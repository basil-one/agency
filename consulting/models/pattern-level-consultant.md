# Pattern-Level Consultant

<p class="consulting-subtitle">Mutual Advantage Model</p>

Pattern-Level Consultants recognize recurring problems, develop transferable approaches, and help organizations apply them. Their independent practice, communities, and relationships create value beyond any single engagement. This model explores how clients, providers, and consultants can each realize greater value by considering their interests together and strengthening the engagement as a whole.

It is useful when conventional engagement terms do not fully account for the value of an independent professional practice. The considerations below are starting points for discussion, not a fixed package of terms or a substitute for applicable contracts and policies.

## 01 The practice and IP boundary

**Why it matters:** Clients gain access to expertise developed across contexts without needing to own the independent practice behind it.

**In practice:** Client-commissioned deliverables and code belong to the client as contracted; pre-existing works and generalizable improvements developed outside scope remain with their rights holders. Independently developed or mined patterns may be published openly under applicable licenses, benefiting everyone—including those who recognize their influence. Independent authorship, open-source work, teaching, and leadership development can all be part of that practice. Protect confidentiality and third-party rights.

## 02 Upstream contributions and communities

**Why it matters:** Community participation refines approaches and gives clients and providers access to specialized knowledge, relationships, and emerging talent.

**In practice:** Independent publishing, open-source work, and professional communities are upstream sources of value. For example:

- **Published practice and source**
  - [Circle3™](https://circle.basil.one), a published practice model, and its [source repository](https://github.com/basil-one/circle).
  - [Agency](https://agency.basil.one) and its [source repository](https://github.com/basil-one/agency), alongside other [Basil One GitHub work](https://github.com/basil-one).
- **Communities and collaborative development**
  - [InnerSource Commons Foundation](https://innersourcecommons.org) and the [InnerSource Commons GitHub organization](https://github.com/InnerSourceCommons).
  - [PLoP](https://plopcon.org), where practitioners develop and refine patterns together.

These are examples of participation, published work, and relevant communities—not claims of endorsement, ownership, or authorship of others' contributions. Applicable licenses and third-party rights remain in force.

## 03 Independent professional activity

**Why it matters:** Publishing, podcast and conference appearances, coaching, and community work bring new problems and perspectives into practice—giving clients broader insight and providers greater capability and reach.

**In practice:** Consultants remain free to pursue these activities outside client duties without routine approval, while respecting confidentiality, resource use, conflicts, and endorsement boundaries. For purchased services, agree scope, fees, schedule, rights, and provider economics separately—for example, an independently offered coaching service. Awareness alone grants no free access or exclusivity.

## 04 Quarterly practice exchanges

**Why it matters:** In-person exchanges deepen relationships, accelerate learning, and connect practitioners with new ideas and opportunities.

**In practice:** One possible arrangement is up to four exchanges annually, approximately quarterly, with one to two days each as agreed. Activities include practice, collaboration, and networking. Benefiting sponsors fund approved participation and reasonable travel; agree time, expenses, and approvals in advance as a visible program investment.

## 05 PLoP sponsorship: an illustrative investment

**Why it matters:** [Pattern Languages of Programs, People & Practices](https://plopcon.org) (PLoP) is a foundational gathering where practitioners across disciplines develop, challenge, and refine reusable knowledge. Participation is fundamental to developing the practice itself—not simply training or demand generation. As AI makes answers and artifacts easier to produce, the human work of recognizing patterns, testing assumptions, and improving thinking and collaboration becomes even more important.

**In practice:** Consider sponsoring annual PLoP participation or an agreed alternative of similar cost. For scale, one practitioner's 2026 all-in PLoP participation cost was under $3,000; this is an illustrative planning reference, not a standard fee. Agree an appropriate budget in advance. Interested clients and providers may attend as guests at reduced rates. Fund approved registration, travel, and lodging separately from consulting rates. Sponsorship may bring acknowledgment, but not authorship, editorial control, or independent IP.

## 06 Transparent engagement-model choices

**Why it matters:** Engagement structures distribute benefits, responsibilities, and risks differently. Clear options help attract and retain specialized practitioners while supporting viable client and provider relationships. When choices feel imposed or better alternatives surface later, frustration and disengagement can distract from strategic priorities.

**In practice:** Compare genuinely available W-2, C2C, direct, and other lawful, client-approved options. Explain compensation, benefits, taxes, insurance, administration, expenses, IP, liability, restrictions, and conversion paths. Make the economics and trade-offs visible, allowing qualified practitioners to choose among available structures so clients and sourcing providers can attract, engage, and retain the expertise they need.

## 07 Sourcing and account-management economics

**Why it matters:** Trusted relationships in specialist communities help clients and providers reach experienced and emerging talent conventional sourcing may overlook.

**In practice:** For requested or mutually authorized sourcing, referrals, vetting, partnerships, and account development beyond client-billable work, agree responsibilities and consideration in advance: paid time, retainer, eligible referral or placement fees, revenue share, or another documented exchange. Unrequested networking is not automatically paid; never bill unapproved work or double-bill.

## 08 Designing for mutual advantage

**Why it matters:** Considering the whole engagement can reveal greater value for every party. Clients gain deeper expertise; providers access specialized talent and opportunities; consultants sustain the practices that make both possible.

**In practice:** Examine the arrangement through each party’s goals and constraints. Explore terms that increase mutual value. When unfamiliarity or tension arises, stay curious rather than bypassing it; those moments can reveal better possibilities.

---

**Reciprocity Threshold:** Would each party endorse these terms if it occupied the other party’s position?
