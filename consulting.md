<section id="hero">
  <h1>Pattern-Level Consulting</h1>
  <p><em>An independent practice. A balanced way to engage.</em></p>
  <p>Some challenges repeat across teams and organizations, even when the circumstances look different. I help people recognize those patterns, develop approaches that can travel, and apply them thoughtfully in their own context.</p>
</section>

<section id="practice">
  <h2>Recognizing what keeps showing up</h2>
  <p>Pattern-level work starts with the situation people are navigating. What is getting in the way? Which tensions or assumptions recur? What has worked elsewhere, and what needs to be different here?</p>
  <p>The contribution might involve facilitation, coaching, mentoring, pattern writing, or developing a learning practice with a team. The form follows the situation rather than a predefined consulting package.</p>
</section>

<section id="independent-practice">
  <h2>Independent practice is upstream</h2>
  <p>My practice continues beyond any single engagement. It develops through professional experience, open-source contributions, pattern publications, coaching, speaking, and participation in communities. Each context contributes to what I notice and what I can share.</p>
  <p>That independent body of work can be brought into a client engagement for learning and application without becoming the client's property. Client-specific deliverables, rights to use existing materials, and confidentiality are matters to agree on clearly.</p>
  <p>You can see threads of this practice in <a href="https://github.com/basil-one">Basil One's public projects</a>, the work of communities such as <a href="https://github.com/innersourcecommons">InnerSource Commons</a> and <a href="https://github.com/Minimum-CD">Minimum CD</a>, and the <a href="https://plopcon.org">PLoP pattern-writing community</a>. <a href="{{ '/know-your-patterns/' | relative_url }}">Know Your Patterns</a> is a separate expression of my independent coaching practice.</p>
</section>

<section id="balanced-engagement">
  <h2>A balanced way to engage</h2>
  <p>A good engagement protects the interests of everyone involved. Clients need clarity about commissioned work, confidentiality, and expected outcomes. Practitioners need room to continue independent authorship, publishing, speaking, coaching, and community participation. Sourcing partners deserve clear commercial arrangements for the services they provide.</p>
  <p>I call this a <strong>Balanced Engagement Model</strong>: make ownership, scope, responsibilities, available contracting options, and the exchange of value explicit. The details belong in a conversation and a mutually agreed arrangement, not in a one-size-fits-all promise.</p>
</section>
